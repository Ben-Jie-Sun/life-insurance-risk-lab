    TWO MAIN METHODS

    Yule–Furry generates arrivals

    With n cumulative candidates, the next birth waits an exponential time of rate λn. Births in month k are admitted at boundary k+1. Departed candidates still contribute to the mathematical acquisition population.

    E[N(t)] = N₀ exp(λt)
    Var[N(t)] = N₀ exp(λt) [exp(λt) − 1]

    Monte Carlo measures uncertainty

    Repeat the same model R times with separate random streams for acquisition, mortality, lapse and returns. Count how many paths ever leave an obligation unpaid.

    Estimated shortfall probability = shortfall paths / R
    Standard error of a sample mean = sample SD / √R

    A 95% Wilson interval describes sampling uncertainty in the estimated probability. An outcome percentile band answers a different question: how widely could individual futures vary?

    Monthly order and supporting contributions

    1. Admit arrivals unless a previous payment shortfall stopped new sales.
    2. Collect premiums and pay expenses, retaining unpaid obligations.
    3. Apply the asset return to available cash.
    4. Draw deaths, then lapses among survivors. At the final covered month, remaining policies expire.
    5. Pay claims; value reserves; check cash and policy conservation.

    Pricing, discrete reserves, dated stress scenarios, exact-paise accounting, replayable inputs and SQLite history remain supporting features. Expiry is the scheduled end of cover; lapse is an earlier exit. This version does not implement individual customer payments, grace notices, fraud detection or investment allocation.

    Boundary of the model

    Zero initial population stays zero without immigration. Unbounded Yule growth eventually becomes unrealistic. Work limits abort an experiment rather than drop difficult paths. Synthetic probabilities, independent policy outcomes and a single liquid investment fund do not establish real-world solvency.
