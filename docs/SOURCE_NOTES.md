# Method and historical source notes

These notes preserve provenance outside the submission PDF, whose references section was removed at the user's request. This project implements established methods; it does not claim to have invented Yule–Furry, Monte Carlo estimation or actuarial valuation.

- Kyle Siegrist, Branching Chains, Random Services: https://www.randomservices.org/random/markov/Branching2.html
- Mustazee Rahman, Continuous-time Markov chains, Durham: https://www.maths.dur.ac.uk/users/mustazee.rahman/markov.html
- Cosma Rohilla Shalizi (2015), Lecture 28: The Bootstrap, Section 3 (Monte Carlo principle), CMU: https://stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/mreg/15/lectures/28/lecture-28.pdf
- Christian Furrer (2023), Introduction to Actuarial Mathematics, University of Copenhagen: https://www.math.ku.dk/bibliotek/arkivet/noter/Intro_act_math.pdf
- WHO (5 May 2022), global excess mortality associated with COVID-19 in 2020–2021: https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2022-14.9-million-excess-deaths-were-associated-with-the-covid-19-pandemic-in-2020-and-2021
- Bank of England, Financial Stability Report (December 2022), LDI resilience and the pension-sector collateral/forced-selling episode: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2022/december-2022

The historical examples motivate stress analysis. They are not reconstructed or used to calibrate this model. The Yule arrival rate and mortality/lapse/return inputs in all bundled examples are synthetic.
